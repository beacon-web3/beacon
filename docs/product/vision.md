# Vision

Beacon is a decentralized discovery network for identifying valuable content before it becomes obvious to everyone else.

The first version focuses on books, but the broader thesis is category-agnostic: as AI makes books, games, films, music, courses, and other media easier to create, the scarce resource becomes human attention and judgment. Beacon exists to make high-quality curation visible, verifiable, and rewarding.

## Problem

Digital discovery is increasingly noisy.

Current systems have structural weaknesses:

* Social feeds optimize for engagement, not long-term value.
* Search works best when users already know what they want.
* Review sites are vulnerable to popularity bias, review bombing, and shallow ratings.
* Recommendation algorithms can amplify what is already popular.
* As AI-assisted creation grows, the amount of content will increase faster than human attention.

The result is a discovery problem: good work can remain invisible while familiar, viral, or heavily promoted work dominates attention.

## Solution Thesis

Beacon introduces conviction-based discovery.

Instead of treating every like or rating as equal, Beacon asks users to put a small amount of value behind recommendations they believe deserve attention. This creates a stronger signal than passive engagement and gives early curators a reason to share quality discoveries.

Beacon combines:

* Economic commitment through locked curator stakes and paid support.
* Reputation through visible discovery history and NFT badges.
* Community curation through transparent ranking and support signals.
* Sustainable funding through treasury inflows, staking yield, and community-approved revenue streams.

## Product Positioning

Beacon should be positioned as a market for cultural discovery, not as a yield product.

The intended message is:

> Build an on-chain reputation as someone who discovers great content before everyone else.

The financial reward layer reinforces discovery behavior, but it should not become the primary reason to use the platform. The strongest long-term asset is trusted curator reputation.

## Long-Term Categories

Books are the MVP category because they are structured, searchable, and culturally rich.

If the book model works, Beacon can expand into:

* Movies
* TV series
* Video games
* Podcasts
* Music
* Educational courses
* AI-generated content
* Independent creator projects

Each category should be added only after the books marketplace validates the core loop.

## Design Principles

* Reward taste, not just capital.
* Prefer transparent mechanics over hidden platform discretion.
* Keep the financial layer understandable and conservative.
* Make treasury and governance activity publicly visible.
* Avoid governance tokens at launch.
* Preserve trust by requiring community approval for new revenue sources.

## Success Hypothesis

Beacon succeeds if users are willing to support recommendations because they believe in the content, their taste, and their public track record, even when financial rewards are uncertain or delayed.

The MVP should test whether people will financially and reputationally back book recommendations they genuinely believe in.
